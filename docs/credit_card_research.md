The credit card rewards landscape is characterized by:
1. **Dynamic Welcome Offers (Sign-Up Bonuses / SUB):** Time-limited customer acquisition incentives that can deliver $200 to over $2,800 in net value upon meeting a Minimum Spend Requirement (MSR).

# Part I: Credit Card Core Terminology & Framework

### 1.1 Sign-Up Bonus (SUB) & Minimum Spend Requirements (MSR)

#### Definitions & Mechanics
* **Sign-Up Bonus (SUB) / Welcome Offer:** A high-value incentive awarded by issuers to new cardholders upon meeting specific criteria within an introductory timeframe. SUBs are the single fastest way to accumulate large point balances.
* **Minimum Spend Requirement (MSR):** The gross dollar amount of eligible net purchases that must settle to the account within the designated period (typically 3 months / 90 days or 6 months).

#### Critical MSR Rules & Pitfalls
1. **The Annual Fee Exclusion:** **The annual fee does NOT count toward the minimum spending requirement.** For instance, on a card with a $6,000 MSR and a $795 annual fee, the cardholder must spend $6,000 on goods and services in addition to paying the $795 fee.
2. **Statement Credits & Returns:** Refunds, merchant returns, and automatic bank statement credits (such as a $300 travel credit or $25 dining credit) that reduce your net balance can cause total net spend to drop below the threshold. If this occurs, issuers will claw back the welcome bonus points or refuse to credit them. Cardholders should build a $200–$500 buffer above the MSR.
3. **Eligible vs. Ineligible Transactions:** Cash advances, balance transfers, lottery tickets, casino chips, peer-to-peer money transfers (Venmo, PayPal friends & family), and gift card bulk purchases (manufactured spending) are flagged by fraud algorithms and excluded from MSR calculations.

#### Issuer Approval Rules & Anti-Churning Policies
* **Chase 5/24 Rule:** Chase will automatically deny applications for almost all credit cards if the applicant has opened 5 or more personal credit cards (across *any* bank, not just Chase) within the previous 24 months. Most small business cards do not add to the 5/24 count, but opening them still requires the applicant to be under 5/24.
* **Chase Sapphire 48-Month Rule:** Cardholders cannot receive a welcome bonus for *any* Sapphire card (Preferred or Reserve) if they received a Sapphire welcome bonus in the preceding 48 months. Furthermore, cardholders may only hold one personal Sapphire card at any given time.
* **Amex Once-Per-Lifetime Rule:** American Express restricts welcome bonus eligibility to once per card product in a lifetime (interpreted practically as once every 5 to 7 years after account closure).
* **Amex "Family Language" Rules:** Amex enforces tier-based hierarchy restrictions. For example, opening the Amex Platinum card first often renders the cardholder ineligible to receive a welcome bonus on the Amex Gold or Green cards later. The optimal path is to apply bottom-up (Green → Gold → Platinum).
* **Amex "Pop-Up Jail":** Amex uses algorithmic screening that alerts applicants during the application process via a pop-up window if they are ineligible for the welcome bonus before running a hard credit inquiry.
* **Capital One 1/6 Rule & 2-Card Limit:** Capital One typically approves at most one personal card every 6 months and restricts consumers to a maximum of 2 personal Capital One-branded rewards cards (excluding co-branded cards). Capital One also pulls credit reports from all three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion).
* **Citi 8/65 Rule & 1/48 Rule:** Citi permits at most 1 card application every 8 days and at most 2 applications within a rolling 65-day window. For the Strata Premier card, applicants cannot receive the bonus if they received a new account bonus for the Premier/Strata line within the past 48 months.
* **Wells Fargo 1/6 Rule:** Wells Fargo limits welcome bonus eligibility to once every 6 to 16 months across its consumer card portfolio.

#### Net Effective Annual Fee Analysis
The true financial cost of a credit card is its **Net Effective Annual Fee**:
$$	ext{Net Effective Fee} = 	ext{Stated Annual Fee} - \sum (	ext{Direct, High-Utility Statement Credits})$$

* **Low Friction Credits:** The Capital One Venture X has a $395 fee, offset by a $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles ($100 floor), creating a **-$5 net effective annual fee**. The US Bank Altitude Reserve features a $400 fee offset by a $325 automatic travel/dining credit (effective fee **$75**).
* **"Coupon Book" Credits:** The Amex Platinum ($895) and Amex Gold ($325) provide credits split across monthly or quarterly buckets (e.g., $10/mo Uber, $10/mo Grubhub, $7/mo Dunkin', $100/quarter Resy). Maximizing these requires active management and only provides value if they offset organic, pre-existing spending.

#### Foreign Transaction Fees (FTF)
* **Standard Surcharge:** Most non-travel cards impose a **2.7% to 3.0% foreign transaction fee** on all transactions settled outside the US or denominated in foreign currencies.
* **The Golden Rule for International Travel:** Never swipe a card with an FTF abroad; a 3% fee wipes out any 1.5% to 2% cash-back earned.
* **Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) Warning:** When paying abroad, international point-of-sale terminals frequently offer to charge in USD instead of local currency. Cardholders must **always choose the local currency**. DCC carries hidden markup rates of 3% to 7% levied by the merchant terminal, which applies even if using a 0% FTF credit card.

### 1.3 Reward Multipliers & Spending Categories

#### Core Spending Categories
* **Dining:** Restaurants, cafes, bars, bistros, fast-food outlets, and food delivery platforms (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub).
* **Supermarkets / Groceries:** Traditional standalone grocery stores (Kroger, Safeway, Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, ALDI). *Exclusions:* Target, Walmart, Costco, and Sam's Club are classified as superstores or wholesale clubs by merchant category codes (MCC) and almost never trigger the grocery multiplier.
* **Travel (Direct vs. Portal):**
  * *Direct Travel:* Bookings made directly on airline, hotel, or car rental websites (e.g., united.com, hyatt.com). Preserves hotel loyalty status, elite qualifying nights, and direct airline customer service.
  * *Portal Travel:* Bookings through issuer engines (Chase Travel, Amex Travel, Capital One Travel, Citi Travel). Typically awards higher multipliers (5x to 10x), but third-party bookings often forfeit hotel elite benefits and complicate rebooking during irregular flight operations.
* **Gas & EV Charging:** Automated fuel dispensers, service station convenience stores, and electric vehicle charging networks (Tesla Superchargers, Electrify America, EVgo).
* **Transit & Commuting:** Rideshare (Uber, Lyft), taxis, passenger rail, subways, public buses, ferries, toll roads, and parking garages.
* **Entertainment & Streaming:** Movie theaters, theatrical productions, concert venues, amusement parks, and digital streaming subscriptions (Netflix, Disney+, Spotify, Hulu, Apple).
* **Catch-All / Unbonused:** General retail, medical bills, insurance, home repairs, utilities, tuition, and services that fall outside bonus buckets.

#### Earning Architectures
1. **Flat-Rate Catch-All:** Consistent 1.5x to 2x (or 2% cash back) on all purchases with no categories to manage (e.g., Capital One Venture X, Citi Double Cash, Wells Fargo Active Cash, Chase Freedom Unlimited).
2. **Tiered Fixed Categories:** Permanent category accelerators that do not rotate (e.g., Amex Gold 4x dining/grocery; Citi Strata Premier 3x dining/grocery/gas/travel).
3. **Rotating Quarterly Categories:** 5% cash back up to a $1,500 quarterly limit in scheduled categories requiring calendar activation (e.g., Chase Freedom Flex, Discover it).
4. **Adaptive Automated Categories:** Automatically identifies your single highest spend category per billing cycle and awards 5% up to a monthly threshold without pre-selection (e.g., Citi Custom Cash up to $500/billing cycle).

# Part III: Web App Implementation Guide & Data Architecture

### 3.1 Data Schema Design (`app/data/cards.json`)
The companion JSON file `projects/credit-card-dashboard/app/data/cards.json` contains a typed array of all 33 cards with the following schema:
* `id`: URL-safe unique slug matching card identity.
* `name`, `issuer`, `network`, `tier`, `category`: Classification metadata for search, filtering, and tabbed views.
* `annualFee`, `authorizedUserFee`: Numbers for mathematical cost calculations.
* `foreignTransactionFee`: Structured object `{ hasFee: boolean, rate: string }` for international travel filtering.
* `rating`: Editorial rating on a 5.0 scale.
* `recommended`: Boolean indicating editor's choice status.
* `signupBonus`: Nested structure with `amount`, `unit`, `spendRequirement`, `timeframeMonths`, `estimatedValueUsd`, and `historicalHigh`.
* `multipliers`: Human-readable descriptions per category for UI badge display.
* `rates`: Normalized numerical multiplier dictionary (`dining`, `groceries`, `flights`, `hotels`, `gas`, `transit`, `streaming`, `catch_all`) for algorithmic comparison and spend calculators.
* `credits`: Array of objects (`name`, `amount`, `frequency`, `description`) enabling net effective fee calculation and interactive checklist trackers.
* `perks`, `rules`, `pros`, `cons`: Arrays of strings for card detail modals and side-by-side comparison tables.
* `rewardProgram`: Detailed object specifying transferability, estimated cpp, cash floor cpp, and top partner programs.

### 3.2 Frontend Dashboard Feature Recommendations
1. **Interactive Net Effective Fee Calculator:**
   Cardholders check off which statement credits they actually use organically. The UI dynamically recomputes the true net cost of the card in real-time.
2. **Category Spend Optimizer:**
   The user inputs estimated monthly spending across Dining, Supermarkets, Flights, Hotels, Gas, and Catch-all. The engine calculates annual point/dollar yields across individual cards and trifecta presets.
3. **5/24 Status & Rules Tracker:**
   Interactive helper where users input their opened account dates to calculate their current Chase 5/24 standing and flag cards with restrictive approval policies.
4. **Side-by-Side Comparison Matrix:**
   Compare 2 to 4 cards across annual fee, welcome offer, multipliers, lounge access, rental car CDW type, and transfer partners.
